# Japan Market Routing

## Route By Hiring Lane

### New Graduate / 新卒

Expect a longer cycle that can include pre-entry, company briefings, Entry Sheets, aptitude or written tests, group discussions, multiple interviews, provisional offer, formal offer, and status-of-residence procedures. Use the current JASSO guide and each company's schedule; never assume one universal calendar.

Produce:

- company list and application calendar
- Entry Sheet question bank
- 履歴書 and academic evidence checklist
- 30/60-second 自己紹介, 学生時代に力を入れたこと, 自己PR, 志望動機
- status-of-residence timeline when relevant

### Second New Graduate / 第二新卒 and Early Career

Check whether the company treats the candidate as new graduate, potential hire, or mid-career. The same person may qualify differently across employers. Emphasize transferable evidence, reason for early change, learning velocity, and realistic level.

### Mid-Career / 中途

Lead with job-relevant evidence, not school chronology. Usually prepare a concise 履歴書 plus a detailed 職務経歴書. Confirm role scope, grade, reporting line, compensation structure, probation, location changes, and start date.

### International / English-Speaking

Separate these questions because they are not equivalent:

- Is Japanese required for interview?
- Is Japanese required for daily work?
- Is the candidate required to live in Japan already?
- Can the employer sponsor or support status-of-residence procedures?
- Can candidates apply from abroad?

Do not infer sponsorship from “international” branding.

## Route By Company Type

| Company type | Typical emphasis to verify | Materials |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Traditional Japanese company | motivation, adaptability, communication, long-term role fit, rotation/transfer | Japanese 履歴書 + 職務経歴書 or ES |
| Global Japanese company | role evidence plus cross-cultural communication | Japanese docs and/or English resume |
| Foreign company in Japan | role evidence, English process, Japan employment terms | English resume; Japanese docs only if requested |
| Startup | scope, speed, ambiguity, business contribution, equity/financial runway | concise resume, portfolio, role-specific stories |

These are hypotheses, not stereotypes. Override them with the actual hiring instructions.

## Language Evidence

Record separately:

- certifications and scores with dates
- interview speaking ability
- reading/writing ability
- ability to perform the target job in Japanese
- domain vocabulary and customer-facing ability

Do not convert JLPT levels into guaranteed workplace fluency. If the JD says “business Japanese,” research what that means for this team and test with a mock interview.

## Residence And Work Status

Capture current status, expiration, activity restrictions, and whether the proposed work appears aligned. Treat eligibility as a verification task, not a scoring preference. Use official Immigration Services Agency pages and professional advice for ambiguous cases.

For a job change, check current notification and status-change requirements. Some statuses have employer- or activity-specific implications; do not generalize from another candidate's experience.

## Live Market Snapshot

For every new campaign, research:

1. current openings by role, location, language and residence requirements
2. salary ranges from at least one official/large-sample source and one role-specific source
3. demand by seniority; do not extrapolate senior English-speaking tech data to juniors or all industries
4. remote/on-site reality and apply-from-abroad availability
5. current visa support wording on actual job pages

Record query, source, sample limitations and date. TokyoDev's international developer survey is useful for its population, but it is not representative of all workers or all software roles in Japan.
