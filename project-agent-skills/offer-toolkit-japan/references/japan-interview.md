# Japan Interview And Story Bank

Read this guide with `interview-question-bank.md`, `interview-claim-boundaries.md` and, for company-specific preparation, `interview-intelligence-refresh.md`.

## Recover Existing Candidate Material

If the user refers to a previous interview pack, do not replace it with a fresh generic script. Search only user-authorized workspace files, attachments, task history or connected documents. Extract:

- confirmed career chronology and corrections;
- questions already encountered;
- statements the user rejected as inaccurate or unnatural;
- unresolved fact checks;
- role-specific wording the user can actually speak.

Map the recovered material to question IDs. Keep all personal facts and answers outside the installed or public skill directory.

## Build A Reusable Bank

Mine only real experiences. Store each story in the user's workspace with situation, individual responsibility, action, result, reflection, evidence, competencies, and Japanese/English versions. Never place personal stories in the installed skill folder.

Prepare at least these coverage areas when relevant:

- ownership and execution
- teamwork and conflict
- ambiguity and decision-making
- failure, feedback and learning
- customer/user impact
- technical or professional depth
- cross-cultural communication
- leadership without authority
- reason for changing jobs and career direction

Also prepare separate evidence for every employer's `why joined`, `what was gained`, `why left`, `what was tried before leaving`, and `what changed in the next decision`.

## Complete Module Coverage

A general Japanese mid-career master pack should cover:

1. self-introduction;
2. career chronology;
3. join/leave reason for every employer;
4. current reason for change;
5. company and role motivation;
6. strength and main achievement;
7. project, team and leadership boundary;
8. development, system, internal-tool and PoC boundary;
9. AI-use and personal-contribution boundary;
10. customer-contact and proposal experience;
11. insufficient experience and hiring value;
12. career plan;
13. strength and weakness;
14. other applications and decision criteria;
15. current industry topic;
16. reverse questions and closing;
17. recovery phrases for unclear questions;
18. lesser-known certification explanation;
19. why each career move was chosen;
20. why the current specialization is continuity rather than reversal;
21. one-minute career consistency narrative;
22. role-specific technical or case questions;
23. compensation, start date, location and residence-status logistics;
24. stage-specific and current `LIVE` questions.

This list deliberately includes short-tenure, leadership, certification and career-consistency follow-ups that a generic STAR bank misses.

## Japanese Answer Shape

For most questions, prefer:

1. **結論**: answer the question in one sentence
2. **理由/状況**: enough context to understand the stakes
3. **具体的な行動**: what the candidate personally did and why
4. **結果**: verified impact
5. **学び/貢献**: what changed and how it connects to the target role

Keep spoken Japanese natural. Do not turn every answer into ceremonial keigo. Correct politeness and clarity are more useful than difficult expressions the candidate cannot deliver reliably.

Do not force every answer into two reasons. Use `結論 -> 事実/理由 -> 自分の担当 -> 結果/限界 -> 応募先との接点`. For important questions, prepare 20-, 60- and 120-second versions.

## Core Modules

### 自己紹介

Prepare 30-second and 60-second versions:

- current professional identity
- one or two relevant strengths with evidence
- concise transition to why this role

Do not recite the entire resume.

### 志望動機

Connect three layers:

1. why this business/company now, based on researched facts
2. why this role, based on the actual JD
3. why the candidate, based on verified evidence and intended contribution

Avoid generic admiration or claims about internal culture without evidence.

### 転職理由 / 退職理由

Be truthful, brief and forward-looking. Do not attack the previous employer. Explain the constraint or desired growth, actions already taken, and why the target role is a coherent next step.

### 日本で働く理由

Use a concrete personal/professional reason and show informed commitment. Do not rely on tourism, anime or vague cultural admiration unless genuinely relevant and connected to work.

### 逆質問

Prepare questions that reveal success criteria, team interfaces, decision rights, onboarding, language use, work conditions and selection next steps. Avoid asking only information already stated clearly on the company site.

### Role And Evidence Boundaries

Before drafting project answers, classify the candidate using `interview-claim-boundaries.md`:

- team member, task owner, main implementer, workstream coordinator, formal PL/PM or people manager;
- study, prototype, PoC, internal tool, production workflow or customer production system;
- direct customer contact, manager-mediated requirements, external coordination or internal stakeholder work;
- human-owned decisions and verification versus AI-assisted steps;
- same-domain certification comparison versus official equivalence.

Use the candidate's highest verified level, not the most impressive label.

### Current And Unexpected Questions

Recent candidate material supports drilling questions about AI's impact on engineering, difficult deadlines, disliked work, leadership difficulties, current news, work motivation, portfolio evidence and the candidate's honest reaction to the interview. Treat these as flexible practice, not guaranteed predictions.

## Mock Interview

1. Select target company, role, interview stage, interviewer type and language.
2. Select `P0`, applicable `P1`, one role overlay and current `LIVE` questions.
3. Ask one question at a time and use the follow-up tree instead of jumping to a new topic.
4. Time the answer and note whether it fits the intended 20/60/120-second version.
5. Score only observable elements: directness, evidence, role boundary, personal action, result, limitation, role fit, language clarity and consistency.
6. Give one priority improvement and ask the same question again.
7. Test pressure points: `why`, `your exact role`, `scale`, `evidence`, `failure`, `what changed`, `why this company`.
8. Update the story bank only after facts are confirmed.

Do not optimize solely for polished language. The candidate must be able to explain artifacts, decisions and limitations naturally when the wording changes.

## Likely Japan-Specific Checks

Depending on lane, prepare for questions about:

- reason for applying and company selection criteria
- education/research and career chronology
- reason for coming to or staying in Japan
- language use in concrete work situations
- cooperation, adaptability and communication
- desired work, location, transfers and start date
- current status of residence and support needs
- every short tenure, career change and employment gap
- exact team size, direct reports and decision rights
- practical language examples rather than qualification level alone
- whether work was production, internal use, PoC or study

These are possible topics, not a license to ask discriminatory or irrelevant questions. Evaluate only role-relevant information and follow fair-hiring principles.

## Consistency Check

Compare answers against the exact submitted 履歴書, 職務経歴書, Entry Sheet and resume. The user should be able to explain every line and number naturally.

Also compare the spoken answer with project artifacts, current company research and any reference-check statement. Run `scripts/interview_pack_audit.py` when a structured pack is available.

## Completion Gate

Do not call the pack complete until it has:

- question-bank coverage by stage and risk;
- one evidence ledger and at least two project cards;
- join/leave logic for every employer;
- explicit leadership, delivery, customer-contact and AI-use boundaries;
- current sources with event dates and tier labels;
- follow-up branches for every high-risk answer;
- natural Japanese versions the candidate can retell without reading.
